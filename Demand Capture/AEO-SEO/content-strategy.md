## 3. Brand voice, tone, and visual identity — see Section 1 for verified visual data.

- Warm + factual. Not corporate, not salesy, not clinical.
- No shame framing — borrowing, including on Centrelink income, is treated as normal.
- Harm-reduction first: free alternatives (NILS, Centrelink advance, hardship arrangements, financial counselling) are presented before any CashLab product pitch.
- Honesty over marketing: the core content differentiator is correcting competitor oversell ("guaranteed approval," "instant $10,000").
- Never name a competitor, anywhere, under any framing.
- American English by default; Australian spelling retained for genuinely local terms/institutions.
- No em dashes. Sentence case, not title case.

## 5. Keyword research — the full journey

### 5.1 Methodology: Path A vs Path B

- **Path A (topic-first):** lock content architecture via competitor teardown + persona mapping first, then extract supporting keywords per post.
- **Path B (keyword-first):** build a wide, unconstrained keyword pool, then let data and scoring determine the final 9 keywords, which then define the posts.

**Path B was the methodology ultimately used**, after an explicit course-correction: an earlier pass had locked 9 post topics first and only then pulled keywords to match them — which meant the "pool of 50 to pick from" framing was hollow, since the picks were already made. Path B was chosen specifically to make keyword selection genuinely data-driven rather than backfilled.

### 5.2 Stage 1 — building and filtering the 100-keyword pool

The 100-candidate pool was built across 17 categories to ensure full coverage of the lending search space: national broad terms, WA-wide terms, Perth-specific, Midland, Geraldton, other WA regional centers, product/amount terms, credit-related terms, income/eligibility terms, life events, urgency terms, debt consolidation (included specifically to test the exclusion), comparison/informational terms, trust/regulation terms, application-process terms, pawnbroking, and Cash Converters displacement terms.

**Stage 1 fit-check removed 12 keywords**, all with logged reasons:

| Reason | Keywords removed |
|--------|---------------------|
| CashLab excludes this product | debt consolidation loan Australia, debt consolidation WA, consolidate credit card debt Australia |
| Side business, not blog focus | pawnbroker Perth, pawnbroker Midland, gold buyer WA, sell luxury goods Perth |
| Off-brand phrasing | payday loan Australia |
| Amount mismatch (avg AU wedding cost exceeds CashLab's $5k ceiling) | wedding loan Australia |
| Near-duplicate of a kept term | personal loans western australia (dup of singular), Centrelink loan Australia (dup of "loan on Centrelink"), best alternative to Cash Converters (dup of "Cash Converters alternative") |

88 keywords survived to Stage 2.

### 5.3 Stage 2–4 — data validation and scoring

Data validation used Google Keyword Planner (volume ranges + competition: Low/Medium/High) as the primary source, since GSC and Google Trends data were requested but only partially returned. Every survivor was also scored against Adam's discovery answers (Section 2) as HIGH / MEDIUM / LOW / NONE alignment.

**Result across the full 88-keyword pool:** 17 scored HIGH alignment, 31 MEDIUM, 40 LOW. Of the final 9 selected, 7 scored HIGH and 2 scored MEDIUM — nothing in the final set scored LOW.

**A genuine finding from this stage:** every hyper-local WA keyword (Midland, Geraldton, Perth, Bunbury) returned Keyword Planner volume of 10 — below the measurable threshold — despite earlier SERP research suggesting some of these (Midland, Geraldton specifically) were "white space" with no real Australian competition. Conclusion reached: local geo intent for CashLab is better pursued through Google Business Profile and service-page SEO, not blog content. This is why no dedicated Midland/Geraldton geo posts made the final Q1 plan, despite the early competitive-teardown research suggesting they would.

### 5.4 The architecture evolution: v1 → v2 → v3

**v1:** An initial 3-pillar structure was proposed (Regional WA, National Australia, Responsible Borrowing), immediately flagged as diluting Q1 focus across too many themes for a 9-post quarter. Collapsed down to recommend a single focused pillar, then reopened once real keyword data was available.

**v2:** Hub/spoke roles were assigned by highest volume + KD within each theme. This produced:
- Pillar A hub = rental bond loan (1,600 vol, High)
- Pillar B hub = $500 loan (720 vol, High)
- Pillar C hub = loan on Centrelink (4,400 vol, High) — unchanged throughout every version

**v3 (locked, final):** Corrected because hub status should reflect topical breadth, not just volume — a hub needs to be the broadest anchor its spokes can plausibly link up to, even though for this account volume and breadth happened to often correlate. This produced two swaps:
- Pillar A: hub moved from "rental bond loan" to **"emergency loans Australia"** (broader topical anchor); rental bond loan became a tightly-fitted spoke instead
- Pillar B: hub moved from "$500 loan" to **"SACC loan"** (the actual regulatory category $500 loan sits inside); $500 loan was dropped entirely to Q2 backlog once MACC loan needed to stay in as an Adam-critical content format, and Cash Converters alternative was prioritized for its displacement tailwind and tight SACC-market fit

**Also decided during v3 tightening (Pillar C):** "Cash Converters alternative" was originally slotted into Pillar C (Centrelink) as a "cross-pillar" spoke, but audited honestly as only a 3/10 topical fit with Centrelink — it's about SACC market dynamics, not Centrelink income. Moved to Pillar B (SACC), where it fits at 9/10 since Cash Converters specifically exited the SACC category. Pillar C's second spoke slot was filled instead by **"loan without payslip"** (70 vol, High comp) — chosen over alternatives "loan for casual workers" (20 vol) and "self employed loan Australia" (10 vol) because it most directly matches CashLab's actual 90-day bank-statement assessment process. Car repair loan was dropped from Q1 to keep Pillar A at a clean 3-post cluster once "appliance loan" and "rental bond loan" were both confirmed as spokes.

## 6. SERP research findings — full detail per hub

Gathered via live manual Google searches (screenshots) plus targeted web research, since this environment's sandbox cannot reach live SEO tools like Firecrawl or Semrush directly.

### Hub A — "emergency loans Australia"

- **Zero genuine blog/article content on page 1 — every organic result is a transactional product page.** Confirmed via direct research including a full page fetch of Mega Cash GO's page (H4-only structure, FAQ accordion, 4x repeated "Apply Now" CTA).
- Ranking: City Finance ($500–$5,000, 12–104 week terms), Fair Go Finance ("Funds in 15 Minutes," 4.9★ 4,000 reviews), Nimble ("Money in 60 Minutes," 4.8★ 1,008 reviews), MoneySmart.gov.au (genuine informational — the only real guide-style content ranking), Good Shepherd NILS (genuine informational), Cash Train (up to $8,000, 4.7★ 46 reviews), Mega Cash GO ($300–$5,000), ClearScore (aggregator).
- **Local Pack present** (map + 3 business listings: Cash Australia, CashnGo Loans, Cashwalle) — Google reads this query with local-commercial intent, not purely informational.
- No AI Overview, no Featured Snippet.
- PAA: "Who can help me with money urgently in Australia?" / "How can I get a $10,000 loan instantly?" / "How can I get a loan urgently?" / "How to get instant emergency loan?"
- Related searches: emergency loans ASAP, ASAP 24/7, australia online, australia no credit check, guaranteed approval australia, $50 emergency loan, ASAP 24/7 australia, australia 24 7.

### Hub B — "SACC loan"

- **AI Overview present**, citing Treasury.gov.au directly (5 sites), structured around a definition plus a "Loan Limits and Costs" section.
- **3 of the top-ranking blogs run near-identical "SACC vs MACC" comparisons** (Friendly Finance, MyCashOnline, Quickzy Finance) — confirmed saturated angle, which directly informed the decision to keep SACC vs MACC brief on the SACC hub and give MACC its own dedicated, deeper post instead.

- Note: search was run from a Delhi, India location, introducing some irrelevant noise results (sacb.co.in) — filtered out of the analysis.

### Hub C — "loan on Centrelink"

- **AI Overview present**, leading with government options first (Advance Payments, Student Start-up Loan) before any private lender — cites Services Australia plus 3 other sites.
- No Local Pack this time.
- **The defining finding: every competitor quotes a different maximum loan amount**, with no consistent logic — Canstar ($2,000), Cash Direct ($200–$3,000), Money3 ($5,000 unsecured / $12,000 secured, 4.8★ 1,212 reviews), Cash Train ($200–$8,000, 4.7★ 88 reviews), City Finance (up to $10,000), Fundo Loans ($500–$10,000, 4.8★ 8,714 reviews — highest review count on the SERP). This inconsistency, cross-checked against CashLab's own application form (Centrelink is 1 of 5 equally-treated income types, no separate product or limit) and Adam's 50/50 customer-split answer, became the entire content angle for this hub: there is no special "Centrelink loan" product, and the inconsistent numbers are lender packaging, not a real rule.
- Government content also ranking: Services Australia (Advance Payment), DSS.gov.au (Loans and Savings, up to $3,000).
- PAA: "Is it possible to get a loan on Centrelink?" / "What is the highest loan you can get from Centrelink?" / "What is the $5,000 cash boost for Centrelink?" / "How can I get a $10,000 instant loan?"
- Related searches: $1,500 Centrelink loan, $1,500 Centrelink loan online, instant cash loans on Centrelink 24/7 without credit check, Centrelink loans same day, emergency cash loans for Centrelink customers, instant loans for pensioners on Centrelink, loans on Centrelink without credit check, can pensioners get a loan from Centrelink online.
- **The "$5,000 cash boost" PAA question could not be verified against a real, current government program** and was deliberately excluded from the published FAQ rather than guessed at.

## 8. Content brief framework and summarized briefs for all 9 posts

### 8.1 The reusable 14-part brief template

Working title · Primary keyword · Meta title (≤60 char) and meta description (≤155–160 char, character-counted not eyeballed) · URL slug (short, deliberately chosen, never auto-generated from the title) · Word count target · Search intent and SERP-feature notes · Target persona · Content angle · Secondary keywords (4–6) · H2/H3 outline with a one-line purpose note per section · Featured snippet strategy (2–3 questions answered in 40–60 words) · Internal linking plan (out and in, named explicitly) · Compliance requirements · Tone/CTA guidance and exclusions · SERP research summary (who's ranking, what they do well, the specific gap this post exploits).

### 8.2 Summarized briefs

**1. Emergency loans Australia (Hub A)** — Persona: Emergency Solver (primary), all personas pass through as pillar anchor. Angle: the only genuinely educational page on a SERP of 100% product pages. H2 sequence: opening scene → what an emergency loan actually is (SACC/MACC unmasking) → how fast you can actually get money → what you can borrow and what it costs (with worked fee table) → the debt-spiral repayment cap → common emergencies → free options before borrowing → how to spot a licensed lender → why CashLab → how to apply → FAQ. **2. Rental bond loan (Spoke A)** — Persona: Life Transition Aussie. ## 9. Blog draft content — what's actually written (3 hubs complete)

All 3 follow the same structural pattern, refined across drafting: short intro (1–2 sentences) → Key Takeaways/Quick Answer box (5 bullets) → table of contents → full H2 body → FAQ (7–8 questions, 40–80 word answers) → mandatory compliance block.

### Emergency loans Australia — ~2,750 words

Key Takeaways covered: "emergency loan" isn't a separate product; fee caps (20%+4%/SACC, $400+48%p.a./MACC); the double-the-loan repayment cap; realistic same-day funding vs "guaranteed in 15 minutes" claims; free options exist. Fee table includes 4 worked SACC examples plus one approximate MACC example. FAQ built directly from the 4 captured PAA questions plus 3 more. Compliance placeholders flagged: ACL/Credit Representative wording, comparison-rate example reference.

### SACC loans explained — ~2,477 words

Key Takeaways covered: SACC definition and range; fee-based (not interest-rate-based) cost structure; the debt-spiral cap; the mandatory 90-day bank statement check; SACC/MACC are different products (pointing to the dedicated MACC post). Fee table includes 4 worked SACC examples, all verified correct via direct arithmetic (20% establishment + 4%/month, checked against $500/4mo=$680, $1,000/6mo=$1,440, $1,500/6mo=$2,160, $2,000/9mo=$3,120 — all matched exactly). SACC-vs-MACC section deliberately kept short given SERP saturation (Section 6). Uses Variant B phrasing from the Facts Library (Section 10).

### Loans on Centrelink — ~2,234 words

Key Takeaways covered: yes you can borrow on Centrelink; no special product exists; the inconsistent competitor maximums explained; government options to check first; red flags around "no credit check" claims. The "$5,000 cash boost" question was deliberately excluded from the FAQ rather than answered with an unverified claim. Uses Variant C phrasing from the Facts Library.

**Known unresolved issue found during client review of the compiled draft:** a designed infographic for the SACC post stated "SACC loans are governed by South Australian credit laws" — factually wrong (SACC is Commonwealth/federal law under the NCCP Act 2009, not a state law). This was introduced during graphic design, not present in the source text, and must be corrected before publishing. See Section 13 for the full lesson.

**MACC worked example flagged for verification:** the $3,000/18-month MACC example (~$4,650 total repayable) does not match a simple flat-rate calculation (which would be $5,560) — it only holds up under a reducing-balance amortization assumption. This needs checking against CashLab's actual repayment calculator before the figure is treated as fact in any published post.

## 12. Compliance — key regulatory facts

- SACC: $300–$2,000, 16 days to 12 months, fees capped at 20% establishment + 4%/month
- MACC: $2,001–$5,000, 16 days to 24 months, fees capped at $400 establishment + 48% p.a.
- SACC debt-spiral cap: total repayment (including all fees) can never exceed double the original loan amount
- 90-day bank statement check is a legal requirement for every SACC/MACC lender, not CashLab-specific
- 10% protected earnings rule applies when more than 50% of a borrower's income is from Centrelink — exact basis (gross vs net income) not fully confirmed, verify against ASIC RG281 before further repetition

## 13. Real mistakes made and corrected (read before repeating)

| Mistake | What happened | Standing rule |
|---------|----------------|-----------------|
| AI-generated infographic stated a factual error | A SACC-post graphic said "governed by South Australian credit laws" — should be Commonwealth/federal (NCCP Act 2009), not state law | Never let an AI image generator render embedded facts, numbers, or regulatory text. Generate images with zero or minimal generic text; add real data as a separate, proofread overlay. |
| URL slugs auto-generated from full titles | Ballooned to 63–82 characters with stop words, one included an invalid apostrophe | Always use the short, deliberately chosen slug from the brief — never auto-generate from the H1 |
| Meta description exceeded the limit | One ran to 173 characters against a 155–160 target | Count characters programmatically before finalizing, don't eyeball it |
| Duplicate content across sibling posts | Non-mandated marketing copy (income-types bullet, application-step bullets) repeated near word-for-word across 2 posts before the Facts Library existed | Built the Facts Library (Section 10); check it before writing any new post |
| A second, subtler duplicate collision | Even after the Facts Library existed, splitting a full-paragraph fact (trust checklist) into individual bullets caused 2 posts to collide on 2 specific bullets (TMD and AFCA) | Track individual bullet-level facts separately from their full-paragraph source when a fact commonly gets split apart |
| Working title changed without permission | A title was revised on an SEO judgment call and regenerated before the client-side owner confirmed | Flag suggested changes and wait for explicit confirmation before applying and regenerating |
| Bundled concept misread | Adam's "Rent or Bond" answer was initially treated as covering only the bond scenario | When client input bundles two concepts with "or," check whether both need separate treatment |
| Unverifiable claim almost published | A PAA-sourced question referenced a possible "$5,000 cash boost" government program that couldn't be confirmed as currently active | Exclude or clearly redirect to an authoritative source rather than guessing at unverifiable current claims |
| Image concept mismatched its section | A "transparent pricing" concept image used a piggy bank (a savings symbol) on a page about loan costs (a borrowing/repayment topic) | Check that a visual metaphor actually matches the semantic content of the section it's attached to, not just the general topic area |

## 16. Adapting this for other channels

The research (Section 5–6), personas (Section 4), brand voice (Section 3), and compliance requirements (Section 12) apply to any channel. What changes by channel:

| Channel | What carries over directly | What needs adapting |
|---------|-------------------------------|------------------------|
| Landing pages | Personas, angle, compliance, tone | Shorter, more direct, benefit-led structure; still needs the full compliance block wherever costs are quoted |
| Social captions | Personas, tone (punchier version), no-shame/no-competitor-name rules | No SEO fields apply; length is platform-specific; harm-reduction messaging may not fit but must never be contradicted |
| Ads | Personas, compliance-critical facts | Tightest space — at minimum the licence reference must be visible/linked if any cost or approval claim is made |
| Email | Full brand voice | Typically shorter and more direct in structure than blog, otherwise same standards |

For any new content idea, on any channel: check it against Section 2 (Adam's answers) and Section 4 (personas) first, use the applicable parts of the Section 8.1 brief template, apply Section 3's voice rules and Section 12's compliance facts without exception, and run it through the Section 13 mistakes table as a final check before publishing.
