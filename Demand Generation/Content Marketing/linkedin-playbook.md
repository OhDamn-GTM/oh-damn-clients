# Min-IT - LinkedIn Playbook

_Governs how Min-IT shows up on LinkedIn across all three posting surfaces. Pair with `Assets/Brand Voice/tone-of-voice-guide.md` (base voice) and the founder persona files (`harinath-persona.md`, `vidya-persona.md`) for surface-specific style. Algorithm-research grounding (Section 0) and workflow structure (Sections 5b, 7–9) adapted from Oh-Damn's own internal LinkedIn playbook (`oh-damn-marketing-os/linkedin-playbook/`)._

---

## 0. What the algorithm actually rewards (grounds everything below)

Real findings, not vibes (see Oh-Damn's internal `research.md` for full sourcing):

- **Personal profiles beat company pages, decisively.** Company pages get only ~1.6–2% organic reach to followers; LinkedIn's ranking system actively favors people over logos.
- **Topical consistency beats breadth.** LinkedIn's 360Brew ranking model builds a "topic DNA" per poster and distributes to interested users outside their network *if* they post narrowly and consistently. Post about everything and you become distribution-invisible - this is why each surface here sticks to its own pillar lane (Section 3) rather than rotating through all of them.
- **Engagement-bait is actively suppressed**, not rewarded, since the March 2026 Authenticity Update: no "Agree? Comment 👇," no coordinated pod-liking, no "link in first comment" workaround. Polls are functionally dead (0.07% engagement).
- **Dwell time, saves, and DM-shares are the real signals** - not likes. A post someone reads for 30 seconds beats one with 50 fast likes. External links cost roughly 60% of reach; native content wins.
- **First 60 minutes after publishing are make-or-break** - LinkedIn tests every post on 2–5% of the poster's network first, and how that group engages decides if it reaches further.
- **Format ranking by engagement:** carousels/document posts highest, then video, then images, then text-only (text struggles to break 2% engagement) - see Section 5b.

## 1. Posting surfaces

Min-IT posts from three surfaces, each with a distinct job. Given the reach gap above, treat the two founder profiles as the primary distribution surfaces and the company page as secondary/supporting - it backs up proof (product milestones, credibility signals) rather than carrying the actual conversation.

| Surface | Voice source | Job | Priority |
|---|---|---|---|
| Harinath (co-founder, full-stack dev) | `harinath-persona.md` | Product/engineering depth: how the platform is built, technical decisions, compliance-by-design | Primary |
| Vidya (co-founder, AWS cloud architect) | `vidya-persona.md` | Infrastructure/trust depth: hosting, security architecture, data residency, operational reliability | Primary |
| Min-IT company page | `tone-of-voice-guide.md` | Brand-level positioning: compliance, heritage, product, industry perspective | Secondary - use it to back up proof (product milestones, credibility, industry recognition), not to carry primary distribution |

Always confirm which surface a post is for before drafting (per `Assets/CONTEXT.md`).

## 2. Cadence

**2 posts per week per surface** (6 posts/week total across all three) - this is Min-IT's deliberate choice, kept as-is regardless of the general "3/week is the growth floor" guidance from algorithm research. Revisit only if engagement data suggests it, not because of the general benchmark.

Stagger surfaces across the week rather than posting all three the same day, so the feed doesn't look like a single coordinated blast. See the content calendar for the actual day-by-day assignment.

**Content buffer:** build each surface's posts at least a few weeks ahead of publishing (the current 4-week calendar is exactly this) rather than drafting the week you publish - this is what prevents the program stalling once initial momentum fades.

## 3. Content pillars

Every post should trace back to one of these. Don't invent a topic that doesn't map to a pillar.

1. **Compliance built in, not bolted on** - the signature positioning. ASIC/NCCP/National Credit Code, audit-ready trails, compliance as architecture not a checklist.
2. **Heritage + independence** - since 2006, Brisbane-based, bootstrapped. The "established but current" paradox (never purely legacy, never purely scrappy startup).
3. **Product depth** - the four modules (Min-IT CRM/origination, Min-IT Pay, Min-IT LMS/servicing, Member Portal) and the application-to-repayment flow.
4. **Persona-led pain points** - manual servicing, reconciliation, stack sprawl, speed to go-live (7–10 days). Centre of gravity: growing non-bank lenders (Persona 2).
5. **Infrastructure and trust** - hosting (Azure, AU region), AU data residency, security posture. Never claim SOC 2 or ISO 27001 as achieved; both are in progress only.
6. **Industry perspective** - commentary on the AU non-bank lending regulatory landscape, without naming or attacking specific competitors.

Pillar-to-surface mapping:
- Company page draws from all six.
- Harinath draws primarily from 1 and 3 (compliance-by-design, product/engineering).
- Vidya draws primarily from 5 (infrastructure, security, hosting, reliability).

## 4. Voice recap (see tone-of-voice-guide.md for full detail)

- Formal-leaning but human. No corporate stiffness, no forced enthusiasm.
- No exclamation marks, no emoji.
- No em dashes.
- Confident, not boastful - let specifics persuade, not adjectives.
- Banned words apply here too: frictionless, seamless, next-generation, AI-powered, best-in-class, world-class, cutting-edge, robust, leverage, synergy, revolutionary, game-changing, unlock.
- Signature phrases ("Compliance built in, not bolted on," "Audit-ready," "Since 2006") should be sprinkled, never repeated twice in the same post or in back-to-back posts across surfaces.

**LinkedIn-specific register (per tone-of-voice-guide.md Section 6):** company page posts open a conversation, not a pitch - earn the meeting through dialogue, not a single hard CTA. Founder posts can be first-person and slightly more personal than company copy, but stay inside the honesty guardrails at all times.

## 5. Format guidance

- Hook line (first 1–2 lines, since LinkedIn truncates) should state the claim or tension plainly - no clickbait, no manufactured drama.
- Body: 3–6 short paragraphs. Vary sentence length; avoid a uniform mid-length cadence.
- One CTA per post, genuine and low-barrier: a real question inviting discussion, an offer to share detail in the comments, or a link only when it adds real value. **Never engagement-bait** ("Agree? Comment below 👇", "Tag someone who needs this") - actively suppressed by LinkedIn since March 2026, and it doesn't fit Min-IT's register anyway.
- No hashtag stuffing - 0–3 relevant hashtags maximum, only if they'd genuinely help discovery (e.g. #NonBankLending, #AustralianFintech). Never force a hashtag block for the sake of format.
- No stock "10x," "game-changer," or listicle-bait formats ("5 reasons why...", "3 things every lender needs..."). If a list genuinely fits the content, keep it to real, specific items, not a manufactured rule-of-three.
- No polls - functionally dead as a format (0.07% engagement).
- Avoid external links where possible; they cost roughly 60% of reach. If a link is genuinely necessary, accept the reach cost rather than working around it with a "link in comments" trick (also penalized).

### 5b. Format choice: text, carousel, or infographic

Format is a per-post choice, not a fixed ratio - pick whatever fits the idea and is realistic to produce. Carousels and infographics get meaningfully higher saves/engagement than text, but only pursue them if they don't become a bottleneck:

- **Text posts** - the default, zero production friction. 150–400 words, short punchy paragraphs (1–2 sentences), hook line first, close with a genuine question or a clear point of view. Best for founder POV, lessons, and behind-the-build content.
- **Carousels** - best for step-by-step frameworks, before/after comparisons, or a worked example (e.g. a fee breakdown, an application-to-repayment flow diagram). 5–10 slides, 1080×1080 (square) or 1080×1350 (portrait), exported as PDF. Strong hook slide up front, one idea per slide, clear CTA on the last slide.
- **Infographics** - single image, one clear insight or data point (e.g. the SACC/MACC fee structure at a glance). Highest-save format when it lands.

If there's no template library set up yet, default to text - a good text post beats a carousel that never gets made.

## 6. Honesty guardrails (non-negotiable, repeated from tone-of-voice-guide.md)

- Never claim SOC 2 or ISO 27001 certification - both in progress.
- Never present US integrations (GoCardless, Usio, PayNearMe, ValidiFI, Accelitas, Zest AI, Equifax US, Argyle, Socure, Mitek) as live.
- Never invent metrics, client names, or testimonials not confirmed in source material.
- Never overclaim ("enterprise-bank transformation," "global banking," "advanced AI underwriting").

## 7. Where the insight comes from (anti-slop rule)

Real insight should come from Harinath's and Vidya's actual work, not be manufactured by sitting down to "think of a LinkedIn post":

- **Harinath** - a real engineering decision, a compliance-by-design tradeoff, a specific feature built, a lesson from building for a regulated industry. Source: what he actually worked on that week/sprint.
- **Vidya** - a real infrastructure or security decision, a hosting/reliability tradeoff, a lesson from building fintech infra. Source: what she actually worked on.
- **Company page** - real product milestones, client-facing proof points, or genuine industry commentary, never a manufactured "trend piece" with no real Min-IT angle behind it.

AI, if used at all in drafting, only helps structure an already-real insight into the hook → body → CTA template - it never generates the insight itself. If a topic on the content calendar doesn't have a real, specific thing behind it by the time it's due to be drafted, swap it for one that does rather than forcing generic content into the slot.

## 8. Workflow

1. Confirm which surface the post is for (company / Harinath / Vidya).
2. Confirm there's a real, specific insight behind the topic (Section 7) before drafting.
3. Draft against the relevant pillar and the surface's persona file, using the hook → body → CTA structure (Section 5).
4. **Review:** a fast, light-touch pass before publishing - tone, brand fit, typos, and factual accuracy if any product/compliance detail is referenced. Minutes, not days; if review is regularly taking longer than same-day, that's a process problem, not a reason to skip it.
5. Run the draft through the Section 8 self-check in `tone-of-voice-guide.md`.
6. Run the draft through the `humanizer` skill before it's presented as done (per `CLAUDE.md`).
7. Log the post's status in the content calendar once drafted/scheduled/published.

## 9. First 60 minutes after publishing (do this every time)

This window determines whether a post reaches beyond LinkedIn's initial 2–5% test audience:

- Reply to every comment with a real response, not "Thanks!" - thoughtful replies carry more algorithmic weight than a generic acknowledgment.
- Proactively comment on 3–5 other relevant posts in the founder's network in the same window - genuine engagement, not reciprocal like-for-like trading.
- Do **not** ask colleagues to like/comment on cue - coordinated engagement reads as a pod and is actively penalized.

---

## 10. Recycling and measurement

- **Recycle what resonates.** If a post clearly gets saves/comments/DM-shares, don't let it die - turn a strong text post into a carousel going deeper, or a follow-up post on the same thread. If a topic keeps resonating across posts, that's a signal it could become longer-form content (a blog post, a case study) later, not just a one-off.
- **Measure what actually matters.** Track saves, comments (and their depth, not just count), and DM-shares per post - these are the signals that indicate real resonance, not raw like counts or impressions. Review periodically: what's working, what to double down on, what to drop.
