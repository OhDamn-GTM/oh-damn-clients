# Min-It — Tone of Voice Guide

_Derived from `Min-It_Cold_Email_Context.md` and `Min-IT_Website_Copy_CONTEXT.md`. Source of truth for brand facts remains Brand_QA — this guide governs how we sound, not what we claim._

---

## 1. Who We Are (the short version)

Min-It is loan management / lending software for Australian non-bank lenders — origination, servicing, payments and compliance in one platform. Founded 2006, Brisbane-based, bootstrapped, 11–25 staff. We are **not** a lender, neobank, BNPL platform, debt collector, generic CRM, or payment processor.

**Brand archetype:** Sage + Caregiver.
**Core paradox to hold in every piece of copy:** trusted heritage (since 2006) + modern rebuild (cloud-native challenger). Never write as either "legacy incumbent" or "scrappy startup" alone — we are both established and current.

**Signature promise:** *"Compliance built in, not bolted on."*

---

## 2. Voice Dials

Use these as a reference scale, not just a vibe.

| Dial | Setting | What that means in practice |
|---|---|---|
| Formality | Formal-leaning, human (not corporate) | Write like a knowledgeable colleague, not a legal disclaimer |
| Confidence | Confident, not boastful | State what the platform does; let specifics do the persuading, not adjectives |
| Plain English | High | Short sentences, everyday words, minimal jargon |
| Concreteness | High | Name the actual module, workflow, or number — avoid abstractions |
| Register | Serious, dry humour at most | No exclamation marks, no forced enthusiasm, no emoji |
| Warmth | Reassuring, hands-on | Sound like a founder-led team that answers the phone, not a faceless vendor |

**One-line test:** if a sentence could be pasted onto a competitor's site unchanged, it's too generic — make it specific to Min-It.

---

## 3. Signature Phrases (use deliberately, don't overuse)

- "Compliance built in, not bolted on."
- "Audit-ready."
- "Configurable."
- "Since 2006."
- "Australian-hosted" / "Australian data residency."
- "Live in [7–10] days."

These are load-bearing phrases — they carry the positioning. Sprinkle, don't repeat the same one twice in one piece.

---

## 4. Banned Words

Never use, in any channel:

> frictionless, seamless, next-generation, AI-powered, best-in-class, world-class, cutting-edge, robust, leverage, synergy, revolutionary, game-changing, unlock

These are generic SaaS filler that undercut the plain-English, specific, trust-led voice. If a sentence needs one of these words to sound impressive, replace the word with a fact instead.

---

## 5. Mechanical Rules

- **No em-dashes.**
- **No emoji**, on any outbound or published copy.
- Avoid unnecessary numerals in outbound sequences unless the number is a genuine differentiator (e.g. "since 2006," "7–10 working days" are fine as established facts; don't manufacture new stats in copy that hasn't been fact-checked).
- Keep sentences short. Prefer plain declarative statements over compound, qualifier-heavy ones.
- Sign-offs on outbound email are first-name only ("Sid"), no title stack, no signature block.

---

## 6. Voice by Channel

Min-It's voice doesn't change identity across channels, but the *register and job of each line* shifts:

### Cold Email
- **Goal:** one reply or a short intro call — one clear CTA per email, always low-barrier ("Reply with a yes").
- Founder "I/we." Address the reader directly as "you / your team."
- Anchored to a specific, real pain (reconciliation, manual servicing, stack sprawl) — not generic value props.
- Proof points are concrete and named (a module, a workflow, a client), never vague superlatives.
- Strengths-first framing, not displacement — we're building trust, not attacking their current vendor.
- **US "efficiency-led" track** (secondary market, used only for `/us` outreach): faster, punchier, ROI- and speed-to-funding-led, "modern challenger" framing, stack consolidation. Reserve this register for US-targeted sequences only — it's a variant, not the default.

### LinkedIn DMs
- Open a conversation rather than pitch — the meeting is earned through dialogue, not a single CTA.

### Website
- More formal / regulatory in register than email — reassuring, audit-ready, industry-fluent.
- Hero-level copy can use light wordplay if it reinforces the compliance positioning (e.g. the *"Seriously Compliant Lending Software"* pun) — but wordplay is the exception, not the pattern; everywhere else, plain and direct wins.
- Persona 2 ("growing lenders") is the default centre of gravity for structure and examples unless a page is explicitly persona-specific.
- AU-led positioning throughout. US integrations and capabilities are referenced only as *in development* — never implied as live or shipped.

## 7. Honesty Guardrails (non-negotiable)

- Never claim **SOC 2** or **ISO 27001** certification — both are *in progress*, not achieved.
- Never present **US integrations** (GoCardless, Usio, PayNearMe, ValidiFI, Accelitas, Zest AI, Equifax US, Argyle, Socure, Mitek) as live — they are *in development*.
- Never overclaim: no "enterprise-bank transformation," "global banking," "advanced AI underwriting," or enterprise procurement credentials Min-It doesn't hold.
- Don't invent metrics, testimonials, or specifics not confirmed in source material — accuracy outranks a punchier line every time.

---

## 8. Quick Self-Check Before Sending/Publishing Copy

1. Does this sound like a confident, hands-on team explaining something true and specific — or like generic SaaS marketing?
2. Any banned words present?
3. Any em-dashes, emoji, or unnecessary numerals (outbound email)?
4. Does every claim trace back to a confirmed fact (not SOC 2/ISO certified, US integrations not live)?
5. Is there exactly one CTA, and is it low-barrier?
6. Could this line have been written about any competitor? If yes, make it more specific to Min-It.
